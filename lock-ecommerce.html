TL;DR

I stepped in as an intern to take over this project after the lead researcher left unexpectedly. With the research methods set and the survey already designed, I partnered with Research Operation to recruit participants, cleaned and analyzed e-commerce data to inform product positioning and competitive analysis, and analyzed survey data to uncover customer needs. I turned the findings into a 24-page deck and presented it directly to the CPG client.

Client's Ask

How could a legacy lock brand stay competitive in a market shifting toward smart locks?

The client, a traditional lock manufacturer, was seeing sales of its mechanical door locks decline as smart locks gained ground.

Challenge

Uncovering both the what and the why with mixed methods on a tight timeline

Recruiting

Partnering with Research Operation on the screener and recruiting message

Screener

Respondents qualified if they:

  • Bought a mechanical door lock online in the past 12 months
  • Did not work in the lock, security or market research industries

Quotas balanced:

  • Gender
  • City tier
  • Household type (single, couple, family with kids, multi-generational)
  • Income level

Recruiting Message:

We’re looking for people who bought a door lock online in the past year to share their experience. The survey takes about 10 minutes, and you’ll receive [incentive] for completing it.

Your responses will be kept strictly confidential and used only for this study. Only the project team will have access to the raw data, which will be kept for two years.

Analyzing

Partnering with Data Scientist on significance testing

Banner Table

Answers to each multiple-choice question were compared pairwise across user segments. A red letter after a number means that column’s percentage is significantly higher than the column with that letter.

For example, 57BC means 57% of under-25s chose feature, significantly more than the 25–34 (B) and 35–44 (C) groups.

Challenge

A table like this runs a large number of pairwise comparisons. At 95% confidence, about 5% of them will come out significant by chance alone.

So a stray significant result or two shouldn’t be over-read. The stronger signal is a consistent pattern, or a result that other data can confirm.

Key Insights

  1. 01Customers prioritize a quiet lock and easy installation.
  2. 02Needs are shaped by the room the lock is used in.
